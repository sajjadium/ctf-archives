Rice writing originated in ancient Anatolia in Turkey and India. Many rituals and rites use rice as a medium, but at some point in ancient Anatolia artisans who were skilled in making miniature paintings decided to turn their skill to making art with what had always been an ancient symbol of prosperity, the oldest example of which lies in Topkapi Palace in Istanbul, Turkey. The artisans would inscribe messages or names on a single grain of rice after it was treated and polished. The text written on a rice grain often depends on the the size of the rice grain. However, sometimes, due to the difficulty of the craft, only one letter can be embedded in a rice grain. Grains that were big and fat were favorites as they offered proper surfaces for writing. However, what is written is on a rice grain is often not just dependent on how big it is, but rather what remains of it. You could say the size of the grain is just how big it appears or how much space it occupies.
